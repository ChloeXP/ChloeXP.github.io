French News Topic Classification — First Version

Fine-tuning two pre-trained Transformer encoders, the French CamemBERT and the multilingual XLM-RoBERTa, to sort Le Monde articles into 13 topics. The dataset had no topic labels, so I built a keyword-based labelling system first, then trained, evaluated and compared both models end to end.

01 · PURPOSE

The question

Automatic topic classification is a basic need for any news archive: routing, recommendation, search. The course asked us to adapt a Transformer fine-tuning notebook to a new language and dataset.

I chose French press articles and framed one question: for a French-only task, does a model pre-trained on French beat a larger model pre-trained on 100 languages? CamemBERT (~110M parameters, French corpus) against XLM-RoBERTa (~270M parameters, 100 languages).

03 · STEPS

What I did, step by step

STEP 1 · DATA
Did
Loaded diverse_french_news from Hugging Face and kept only Le Monde articles with a title, description and body.
Why
One newspaper means one editorial style, so differences between models are not blurred by source effects.
Result
17,980 / 538 / 550 articles for train / validation / test.
STEP 2 · LABELS
Did
Defined 13 topics (Monde, Europe, Faits-divers, Politique, Société, Environnement, Sport, Culture, Éco/Conso, Santé, Sciences & Tech, Météo, Jeux) with 21–32 keywords each, and scored every article: +3 per keyword in the title, +1 in the title or description.
Why
The dataset has no topic column. The title carries the editorial focus, so it gets more weight.
Result
Strong imbalance: Monde 22% and Politique 20% of training articles, Jeux only 1% — a 22:1 ratio.
STEP 3 · SAMPLING
Did
Drew a balanced subsample: up to 384 training articles per class (4,784 in total), with the same rule applied to validation and test.
Why
Keep GPU time within a Kaggle session and stop the model from simply favouring the two largest classes.
Result
12 classes at 384 articles, Jeux capped at its 176 available; 373 test articles.
STEP 4 · MODEL
Did
Wrapped each pre-trained encoder in a small classifier: the [CLS] embedding, dropout 0.3, then a linear layer to 13 classes. Input: title and description, up to 256 tokens.
Why
The same head on both encoders makes the comparison about the pre-training, not the architecture.
Result
One reusable TransformerClassifier plus shared train_model and evaluate functions.
STEP 5 · TRAINING
Did
Fine-tuned both models with AdamW (lr 3e-5, batch 16): a first run of 3 epochs, then a second run of 5 epochs.
Why
The 3-epoch learning curves were still rising, with no sign of overfitting.
Result
CamemBERT's macro-F1 went from 0.690 to 0.719, XLM-R's from 0.648 to 0.686.
STEP 6 · ANALYSIS
Did
Per-class precision / recall / F1, confusion matrices and the most frequent confusion pairs for each model.
Why
One accuracy figure hides which topics fail and why.
Result
The weakest classes are the smallest ones (Météo, Jeux), and most errors involve the catch-all Monde class.

Reading the results

  • French pre-training paid off: CamemBERT led in both runs, on 9 of 13 topics, with 2.5× fewer parameters.
  • Small classes fail first: Météo and Jeux, with 14 and 7 test articles, are the only topics below 0.5.
  • Monde is a catch-all: it appears in most confusion pairs, a first hint that the labels themselves were blurred.
A working end-to-end fine-tuning pipeline, and a first answer: CamemBERT > XLM-R on French news.
05 · LESSONS

What a later review found

Coming back to this version, I questioned the numbers before trusting them. Three weaknesses stood out, and each one became a workstream of the revised project.

LABELS

The ground truth was a heuristic

The models were trained to imitate the keyword rule, not the real topic. Articles that matched no keyword silently defaulted to Monde, which explains its role in so many confusions.

In the revision: labels rebuilt from the editorial section in each URL. The keyword rule agreed with the editors on only 39% of articles.

EVALUATION

Too little evidence for the gap

373 test articles, 7 of them for Jeux, and a single seed per model. A 2–3 point gap between two models is within the noise of such a setup.

In the revision: a 1,308-article test set, 3–5 seeds per setting and paired statistical tests.

CODE

The second run did not do what it said

The 5-epoch run set the training size to all 17,980 articles, but the data loaders were still built from the 4,784-article balanced sample. The gain came from extra epochs, not extra data.

In the revision: one run_experiment(config) function and every reported number generated from saved run logs.
